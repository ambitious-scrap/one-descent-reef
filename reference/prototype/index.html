The light thins. The story doesn’t.

Every ten meters down, the ocean keeps more of its secrets. The reef holds most of them.

There. On the wall.

Meet the city that builds itself.

Coral is an animal, a builder, and a landlord to thousands. It has been growing this wall for four hundred years.

The Antler — staghorn coral

An animal colony, not a plant. Its branches grow up to 10 cm a year and shelter juvenile fish by the hundred.

The Dome — brain coral

Centuries old and slow-growing. One dome can outlive every human who ever swims past it — if the water lets it.

The Fan — gorgonian sea fan

It faces the current to feed, filtering plankton with a living lace older than most trees on shore.

Follow the turtle. Notice the water.

The water is changing.

A few degrees of sustained heat, and coral expels the algae that feed and color it. What’s left is white — alive, but starving.

This is the same reef.

When heat stays, color leaves.

This coral is not dead. It is holding on — and it can come back, if the water lets it.

Touch the coral. It remembers.

Look down. Someone’s here.

Recovery isn’t a miracle. It’s a method.

Fragments of surviving coral, grown on lines, planted back by hand. REEF volunteers have done this ten thousand times.

Tag 041 — staghorn fragment

Planted 14 months ago from the survivor patch. Now 9 cm and hosting its first damselfish.
